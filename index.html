PILA'26: Personal Intelligence in Agentic AI Era
KDD 2026 Workshop

August 9–13, 2026 · Jeju, Korea · Held with KDD 2026

About The Workshop

Large language models (LLMs) and agentic AI systems are rapidly moving into user-facing applications, yet most remain fundamentally generic and optimized for population-level objectives. PILA'26 is motivated by the need to move beyond one-size-fits-all models toward personal intelligence: AI systems that explicitly model individuals and dynamically adapt their reasoning, behavior, and decisions through memory, interaction, and lifelong learning. The workshop brings together researchers and practitioners from data mining, LLMs, NLP, IR, human-centered AI, and AI safety to position personalization as a core direction for next-generation AI at KDD. Topics include user modeling and personalized alignment, self-evolving memory and continual learning, datasets/benchmarks/evaluation protocols, real-world applications such as personal assistants and personalized healthcare, and privacy, safety, and trustworthiness in user-adaptive AI systems. Through invited talks, contributed papers and posters, and panel discussion, PILA'26 aims to consolidate key challenges and future research directions for personalized LLM and agentic systems.

Call For Papers

We solicit submissions that advance research on personal intelligence in the era of agentic artificial intelligence. This page summarizes important deadlines, manuscript preparation rules, the review and publication policy, and representative topics of interest.

Key Dates

  • Workshop website & CFP published: April 1, 2026 (AOE)
  • Paper submission deadline: April 30, 2026 (AOE)
  • Author notification: June 4, 2026 (AOE)
  • Camera-ready deadline: June 22, 2026 (AOE)

Unless stated otherwise, all deadlines are interpreted under the Anywhere on Earth (AOE) convention (i.e., 23:59:59 on the stated calendar day in the AOE timezone).



Formatting Requirements

Submissions must be written in English and prepared in the ACM two-column proceedings format, consistent with the official ACM proceedings templates and author instructions: ACM proceedings template · ACM SIG Proceedings template on Overleaf.

For LaTeX users, compile with the acmart class using anonymized review settings—for example: 

\documentclass[sigconf,anonymous,review]{acmart}

Anonymized review copy. The PDF submitted for review must be fully anonymized for double-blind peer review: omit author names, affiliations, and acknowledgments; avoid links or wording that identifies authors or institutions; cite your own related work in the third person where needed; and anonymize any supplementary files that may be distributed to reviewers. Camera-ready versions may restore full author information after acceptance.

The main paper (excluding references and optional appendices) must be 4–8 pages in length. Submissions must be uploaded as PDF.

Optional supplementary material. You may attach a separate supplement (e.g., proofs, pseudo-code, or reproducibility artifacts). The supplement has no fixed page limit and may be single- or double-column; reviewers are not required to read it. The main paper must be self-contained.

Submission Site & Review Policy

Submissions and reviews are handled via OpenReview. Submission portal: https://openreview.net/group?id=KDD.org/2026/Workshop/PILA.

Review model. PILA conducts double-blind peer review: reviewer identities are not disclosed to authors, and author identities must not be inferable from the submission during review (see anonymization guidelines above). Program committee members will follow ACM-style conflict-of-interest procedures when assigning and handling reviews.

Relationship to prior work. Papers may extend authors’ earlier publications when the manuscript clearly articulates what is new, cites prior work appropriately, and accurately represents the contribution. Parallel or overlapping submissions remain subject to KDD 2026 workshop rules and to the policies of any other venue involved; authors are responsible for compliance and for any required disclosures.

Publication model. Accepted papers are non-archival. As with several co-located KDD workshops, camera-ready versions will be posted on this workshop website only and are not included in the official KDD 2026 conference proceedings. Authors remain responsible for complying with venue-specific policies when submitting revised or extended versions elsewhere.

Best Paper Award

The program committee will select one Best Paper from among accepted submissions. Decisions will be based on widely used scholarly criteria, including originality, technical soundness, clarity of presentation, and potential impact on the workshop community.

In-Person Program (KDD 2026)

PILA will be held in person as part of KDD 2026. The program is designed to support technical exchange through invited talks, contributed oral presentations and posters where applicable, and panel discussion, together with informal interaction among participants.

Topics of Interest

The workshop will cover a range of topics, including but not limited to:

(1) Personalized Agent Understanding and User Modeling:

This topic focuses on how personal AI agents build robust user models from preferences, behavior, and evolving context. We will discuss profile grounding, preference drift, uncertainty-aware personalization, and benchmark design for long-horizon user understanding.

(2) Agent Memory, Retrieval, and Knowledge Integration:

This topic explores memory architectures and retrieval strategies for personal intelligence, including episodic memory, semantic memory, context compression, and hybrid retrieval-generation pipelines that support reliable long-term agent behavior.

(3) Adaptive Planning and Tool Use for Personal Agents:

This topic investigates reasoning and action strategies that let agents plan over user goals and constraints. We welcome work on tool routing, multi-agent collaboration, self-correction loops, and adaptive control policies for dynamic real-world tasks.

(4) Evaluation, Alignment, and Safety in Personal Intelligence:

This topic focuses on trustworthy personal AI: user-aligned objectives, controllability, transparency, reliability under distribution shift, and robust evaluation protocols that reflect real user outcomes instead of proxy-only metrics.

(5) Privacy, Deployment, and Human-AI Interaction:

In addition to core modeling topics, we welcome research on privacy-preserving personalization, on-device and federated approaches, interaction design for human-agent collaboration, and deployment experiences for personal AI in production settings.
