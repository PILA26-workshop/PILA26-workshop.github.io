PILA 2026
Personal Intelligence in the Agentic AI Era

ACM SIGKDD Conference on Knowledge Discovery and Data Mining (KDD) 2026 · 9–13 August 2026 · Jeju, Republic of Korea
Co-located workshop

About the Workshop

Large language models (LLMs) and agent-based systems are increasingly deployed in applications that serve individual end users; many systems nonetheless remain tuned to aggregate objectives rather than to specific people. PILA 2026 focuses on personal intelligence: models and agents that represent individual users and adapt inference, actions, and decisions over time through memory, interaction, and continual learning.

The workshop brings together researchers and practitioners across data mining, machine learning for language and information access, human-centered computing, and trustworthy AI. Scope includes user modeling and personalized alignment; memory, retrieval, and lifelong adaptation; datasets, benchmarks, and evaluation methodology; applications such as personal assistants and health-oriented assistance; and privacy, safety, and reliability in user-adaptive systems. The technical program comprises invited presentations, contributed talks and posters, and a panel discussion oriented toward open problems and research agendas for personalized LLM and agent-based AI.

Call for Papers

We invite original research submissions addressing personal intelligence for large language models and agent-based systems. This section specifies submission deadlines, manuscript preparation, the review and publication model, and indicative research topics.

Key dates

  • Workshop site and CFP 1 April 2026 (AOE)
  • Paper submission 30 April 2026 (AOE)
  • Notification of acceptance 4 June 2026 (AOE)
  • Camera-ready papers 22 June 2026 (AOE)

Unless otherwise noted, each deadline ends at 23:59:59 Anywhere on Earth (AOE) on the indicated date.



Manuscript preparation

Manuscripts must be written in English and formatted in the ACM two-column proceedings style, following the official ACM proceedings templates and author instructions: ACM proceedings templates · ACM SIG Proceedings template (Overleaf).

LaTeX

Use the acmart class with the anonymous and review options, for example: 

\documentclass[sigconf,anonymous,review]{acmart}

Double-blind review and author anonymity

Review is double-blind. The submitted PDF and any materials distributed to reviewers must not disclose author or institutional identity. Remove names, affiliations, and acknowledgments where they identify authors; cite and discuss prior work—including the authors’ own—in a manner consistent with standard anonymization practice; and avoid hyperlinks, artifact references, or supplementary files that lift anonymity. After acceptance, the camera-ready PDF may restore complete author and affiliation metadata for publication on this site.

The paper body (excluding references and optional appendices) must be 4–8 pages. Upload the paper in PDF through the submission system.

Optional supplementary material. Authors may upload an appendix or separate supplement (e.g., proofs, pseudocode, code, or data descriptors). Length is not restricted; layout may be single- or double-column. Reviewers are not obliged to consult supplemental material. The main paper must stand on its own.

Submission and review

Submissions and reviews are managed in OpenReview. Submission portal: https://openreview.net/group?id=KDD.org/2026/Workshop/PILA.

Review model. PILA employs double-blind peer review: reviewer identities are not revealed to authors, and author identities must not be deducible from the submission during review (see above). The program committee applies ACM-style conflict-of-interest rules for reviewing and decisions.

Relationship to prior work. Submissions may extend earlier publications by the same authors when incremental contributions, citations, and positioning are explicit and accurate. Parallel submission and overlap with other venues are governed by KDD 2026 workshop rules and by each venue’s policy; authors must comply and disclose as required.

Publication model. Accepted papers are non-archival. Camera-ready versions appear on this workshop website only and are not part of the official ACM KDD 2026 conference proceedings. Authors who submit extended work elsewhere remain responsible for satisfying those venues’ policies.

Best Paper Award

The program committee may designate one Best Paper among accepted submissions, based on originality, technical quality, clarity, and expected impact for the workshop audience.

Workshop format

PILA is an in-person workshop co-located with KDD 2026. The program comprises invited talks, contributed oral presentations and posters as allocated, and a panel session, with time for discussion among participants.

Topics of interest

Representative areas include, but are not limited to, the following:

(1) Personalized agent understanding and user modeling

User and context models for personal agents: preference and behavior signals, profile grounding, drift and uncertainty, long-horizon understanding, and evaluation design for realistic user settings.

(2) Memory, retrieval, and knowledge integration

Architectures and methods for personal agents: episodic and semantic memory, context compression, retrieval-augmented generation, and hybrid pipelines for stable long-horizon behavior.

(3) Planning, tool use, and multi-step agents

Reasoning and execution under user goals and constraints: tool selection and routing, multi-agent coordination, self-correction, and policies for dynamic or open-ended tasks.

(4) Evaluation, alignment, and safety

Measurement and guarantees for personal AI: alignment with user objectives, controllability and transparency, robustness under shift, and metrics tied to downstream outcomes rather than proxies alone.

(5) Privacy, deployment, and human–AI interaction

Privacy-preserving and on-device personalization; federated and distributed settings; interaction design for human–agent workflows; lessons from production deployment and operations.
