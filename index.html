PILA'26: Personal Intelligence in Agentic AI Era
KDD 2026 Workshop

About The Workshop

Large language models (LLMs) and agentic AI systems are rapidly moving into user-facing applications, yet most remain fundamentally generic and optimized for population-level objectives. PILA'26 is motivated by the need to move beyond one-size-fits-all models toward personal intelligence: AI systems that explicitly model individuals and dynamically adapt their reasoning, behavior, and decisions through memory, interaction, and lifelong learning. The workshop brings together researchers and practitioners from data mining, LLMs, NLP, IR, human-centered AI, and AI safety to position personalization as a core direction for next-generation AI at KDD. Topics include user modeling and personalized alignment, self-evolving memory and continual learning, datasets/benchmarks/evaluation protocols, real-world applications such as personal assistants and personalized healthcare, and privacy, safety, and trustworthiness in user-adaptive AI systems. Through invited talks, contributed papers and posters, and panel discussion, PILA'26 aims to consolidate key challenges and future research directions for personalized LLM and agentic systems.

Submission Format

All papers must be submitted in PDF format, using the KDD 2026 workshop submission format (details coming soon). We welcome two types of papers:
Full papers: Full-length research papers from 4 to 7 pages (excluding references and appendices).
Short papers: Research/position papers of up to 4 pages (excluding references and appendices).

Submission Site

The workshop uses OpenReview for paper submission and review. The submission link will be announced soon.
The review process will be single-blinded, and we welcome accepted and published papers. The contributions will be non-archival and only will be hosted on our workshop website. There will be one best paper award for accepted papers with outstanding quality.


Topics of Interest

The workshop will cover a range of topics, including but not limited to:

(1) Personalized Agent Understanding and User Modeling:

This topic focuses on how personal AI agents build robust user models from preferences, behavior, and evolving context. We will discuss profile grounding, preference drift, uncertainty-aware personalization, and benchmark design for long-horizon user understanding.

(2) Agent Memory, Retrieval, and Knowledge Integration:

This topic explores memory architectures and retrieval strategies for personal intelligence, including episodic memory, semantic memory, context compression, and hybrid retrieval-generation pipelines that support reliable long-term agent behavior.

(3) Adaptive Planning and Tool Use for Personal Agents:

This topic investigates reasoning and action strategies that let agents plan over user goals and constraints. We welcome work on tool routing, multi-agent collaboration, self-correction loops, and adaptive control policies for dynamic real-world tasks.

(4) Evaluation, Alignment, and Safety in Personal Intelligence:

This topic focuses on trustworthy personal AI: user-aligned objectives, controllability, transparency, reliability under distribution shift, and robust evaluation protocols that reflect real user outcomes instead of proxy-only metrics.

(5) Privacy, Deployment, and Human-AI Interaction:

In addition to core modeling topics, we welcome research on privacy-preserving personalization, on-device and federated approaches, interaction design for human-agent collaboration, and deployment experiences for personal AI in production settings.
